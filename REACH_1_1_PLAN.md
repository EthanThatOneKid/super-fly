## Acceptance gates

1. The teacher trajectory must reach the Level 1-1 flagpole and hold the completion state for 30 frames.
2. `eval_harness.py` must report `completed` and `completion_rate`; max X is not success.
3. Compare model-only, right-only, bootstrap-only, and teacher upper-bound policies with matched reset, horizon, and seed.
4. Freeze the earliest checkpoint that passes the model-only completion gate. Do not call bootstrap success learning.

- **Gate-eligible real-ROM run** (container build, cadence 15, settle 5, horizon 1600, one episode on each of seeds 42/43/44), `env_kind: stable-retro`, `model_only: true`, `p0_gate_met: false`: untrained baseline best_x 898 / mean 504.0; DAgger round 1 best_x **1247** / mean 875.7 (2.1x the mainline temporal-decoder reference of 594, 1.4x its own untrained baseline); round 2 continued from round 1 and regressed to 899. No episode completed in any arm. The teacher plan itself completes the level in 1,477 frames (max_x 3243, no death), so both the upper bound and the completion detector are real.
- Two decoder defects blocked the gate and are fixed: the jump evidence was summed so that the jump channel cancelled itself out of the comparison (a learned jump read as a tie, so the controller never jumped and died at the first obstacle), and the jump margin was hard-coded to zero on a readout whose evidence is offset for both chunk classes. Calibrating the margin from labelled evidence moved round-1 best_x from 313 to 1247. Supervising every frame rather than one target per cadence chunk was tested and is worse (decision-point separation 0.24-0.29 versus 2.4-5.8).
- Known ceiling, now measured against a null arm: pretraining only fits the `layer3_4` readout over a fixed random connectome, and the bounded-rate pre-screen puts the two rounds at **0.496** and **0.504** jump-chunk recall inside a 0.35 jump budget on the dev seeds, against **0.360** for an untrained model and 0.343 for chance (the reserved three gave 0.413 / 0.427 against 0.333). The earlier "~0.66-0.70 calibrated balanced accuracy" claim was measured at 10-30 epochs; at the epochs the runner uses it is 0.541, which is why the pre-screen no longer uses balanced accuracy. Level 1-1 needs 100 sequential decisions with no fatal misjudgement, so a 15-16 point edge over chance cannot chain into completion. Raising this ceiling requires a decision that actually separates the teacher's jumps, not another DAgger round.
- **The decision evidence was measured against its own ceiling** (`oracle.py`), to price the two remaining candidates: the rule that reads the motor spikes, or the representation underneath it. Real shard, 99 chunks (25 jumps), dev seeds 45-49, threshold *and* timing chosen from the labels, all quoted at bounded jump cost (run-chunk recall >= 0.95). Bounded per-jump recall, AUC in brackets: untrained 0.440 (0.844) at settle 5, 0.624 (0.936) at 10, **0.952** (0.990) at 20; round 1 0.656 (0.933) / 0.808 (0.978) / 0.928 (0.992); round 2 0.648 / 0.800 / 0.944; visual-pathway arm 0.000 (0.500) at every window up to 10. Three readings, and the pre-committed 0.97 bar is cleared by none of them, so the verdict is `representation` everywhere: (a) the evidence is *starved, not saturated* -- the ceiling climbs monotonically with the number of draws per decision, and at 20 draws the classes are ordered at AUC 0.99; (b) the shipped `sum` wastes most of what its own window holds -- 0.056 bounded recall and AUC 0.657 where the family ceiling at the same window and the same draws is 0.656 and 0.933; (c) the population one layer upstream does not rescue it -- a held-out one-dimensional readout of the central complex reaches 0.448 (settle 5) and 0.168 (settle 20), and a 1-NN probe 0.200 / 0.152, both *below* the motor ceiling, while the same readout fitted in sample scores 1.000 (128 dimensions against ~500 frames, which is a fit and not evidence). Consequence: the cheapest uncovered margin is a decoder change (more evidence per decision, and a non-saturating statistic), and what remains after that is a representation question. See "Where the ceiling is" in the README.
- **The trained readout is the only thing any arm has changed so far.** Re-recording with identical seeds shows the central-complex population is **bit-identical** across the untrained baseline, DAgger round 1 and round 2: they differ only in `layer3_4` (512 weights over 128 central units), because the connectome below the readout is frozen and nothing downstream feeds back into it. The one arm that does change the population (visual-pathway training) saturates it -- its motor layer emits no spikes at all (`motor mean 0.0`, AUC 0.500), which is the source of the degenerate always-jump calibration the sequence gate's rate budget had to catch.
- **Recovery targets were matched on progress only, which taught the wrong action wherever the teacher was airborne** (`dagger.py`). On the recorded ROM shard the teacher takes 43 of its 74 run decisions mid-flight, so progress-only matching mislabels **1159 of 3244 ground states (35.7%)** as `run`; every one of those disagreements is `run` -> `jump`, i.e. a missing jump the teacher actually performed. At the x 594 stall it labelled `run` from the teacher's mid-flight frame there instead of the jump at x 549 that clears the pipe. Matching on (progress, ground/airborne phase) recovers the takeoff chunk, and a jump target is now held for the remainder of its teacher chunk so a flight in progress is not relabelled mid-air. Reporting: `teacher_labelling` in every round report, plus `--label-matching position_only` to ablate the fix on identical rollouts.
- **Ablating that fix rules it out as the gate blocker.** Same seeds, cadence 15, settle 5, horizon 1600, 30 epochs: the model-only rollout visits the same 131 frames and dies at the same x 312 in both arms, so only the window's supervision differs. `position_only` labels the window `run` from chunk start x 287; `phase` labels it `jump` from x 249 and every one of the window's 9 frames was previously mislabelled. Closed-loop round 1: best_x **1247 vs 1246**, per-seed 700/680/1247 vs 700/680/1246, motor argmax accuracy 0.647 vs 0.643. Nine corrected frames out of 1486 do not move a ~0.64-accuracy readout, so the labelling defect is a correctness fix that would compound in a longer-surviving run, not the binding constraint. The hypothesis that mislabelled recovery windows explained round 2's regression is falsified.

   That view was then replaced in turn, because it still scored one decision at a time from a cold SNN state, ignoring the decoder's chunk commitment and the refractory period, with no notion that a missed jump ends the run. The default is now a **teacher-forced sequential replay** (`offline_episode.py`): the teacher's frames are fed in order through the same decoder the closed loop drives, and an arm is scored by `jump_sequence_recall` (of the 20 jumps the teacher actually took, how many the controller took, decided in sequence). See "The pre-screen: how an arm earns an emulator run" in the README.
6. ~~Split the seeds iteration happens on from the seeds the claim is reported on.~~ Done: `seed_policy.py` reserves 42/43/44 for the closed loop's model-only evaluation and moves every fitting, sweep and verdict to the dev range 45-49. 7. Re-read what the sequential gate can predict, now that it is measured on the tuning seeds. The published table was on the seeds the arms were being selected against, and moving to the dev five (5 replays instead of 3) **flips the round-1/round-2 ordering**: 0.840 vs 0.810 with round 1 ahead, matching the emulator's 1247 vs 899, where the reserved three had round 2 ahead at 0.817 vs 0.850. What survives both sets: `best_x` prefers round 2 where the emulator has it behind, per-replay spread exceeds every arm's paired gain, and nothing passes either way.
8. ~~Give the decision more evidence before touching the network: replace the statistic.~~ **Tried and rejected.** The ceiling priced two decoder levers on identical draws -- the statistic shape (0.056 -> 0.656 bounded recall, AUC 0.657 -> 0.933) and the number of draws (0.656 -> 0.928, AUC 0.933 -> 0.992) -- so the statistic was made a parameter (`evidence.py`: eight settlement-window rules, the shipped `spike_sum` among them, the rule and its decay carried in the checkpoint, the margin re-fitted and its provenance recorded when a checkpoint's threshold was fitted for another statistic) and then measured against the gate on the dev seeds with `--evidence-rule` forced across every arm, baseline included. It buys nothing that is rate-free: budget-view recall barely moves across five statistics -- `spike_sum` / `spike_leaky_recency` @ 0.25 / `drive_sum` / `drive_leaky_recency` @ 0.25 / @ 0.75 give untrained 0.360 / 0.344 / 0.336 / 0.336 / 0.336 and round 1 0.496 / 0.480 / 0.488 / 0.480 / 0.544 -- which is what the AUC already said (the drive orders the chunks no better, 0.611 against 0.657), while the sequential view swings by 0.30 on every arm (untrained 0.660-0.960, round 1 0.510-0.840, round 2 0.440-0.750) because the calibrated margin lands on a different operating point per statistic and the arms that look best are the ones spending the most: the untrained arm's 0.960 appears twice, at jump rates 0.374 and 0.404, both over the 0.35 budget, where its own budget-view recall is 0.336 -- the same as the rules it appears to beat. The sequential ordering is no more stable than it was between the DAgger rounds: `spike_leaky_recency` puts round 2 ahead (0.750 vs 0.720) where the shipped sum puts round 1 ahead (0.840 vs 0.520). No arm passes under any statistic, `spike_sum` stays the default and the drive rules stay reachable. **So the next arm is the window length**, the only lever found that moves a ceiling near the 0.97 completion needs (settle 20: 0.952 untrained, 0.928-0.944 trained) -- a different controller at 4x the inference cost, offline-provable first and then re-measured closed-loop.

   **What the statistic replacement did expose is a defect in the paired criterion.** A baseline's margin is calibrated like an arm's, but nothing constrains the *rate* it spends, so with the flat drive the untrained baseline jumps on 37.4% of decisions -- above the 35% budget -- and every trained arm then "fails" `beats_untrained` (-0.22, -0.52) against a bar that overspends the budget the arm is held to. No verdict flips (both arms fail completion and recall on their own merits), but this is the always-jump exploit one level up and it needs fixing before the paired criterion is trusted again.
9. Only then sweep the cadence (`1, 4, 8, 15`) and recovery-window budgets (`0, 4, 8, 16`) on the dev seeds, and use the jump-rate-matched calibration (`method="jump_rate"`) as a second arm, to see whether the residual spread is cadence, window count, or threshold placement.
10. **Give the replay consequences, or stop asking it to predict dynamics.** Teacher forcing is what stopped the sequential pre-screen ranking the arms: the frames belong to the teacher, so the arm's own trajectory never exists and jumping constantly is unpunished (measured: one checkpoint scores a perfect recall and a completion while spending 49.5% of its decisions jumping). The cheap path is to keep it as a gate and a diagnostic -- which is what it now is, and what caught the exploit. The real path is to model what a jump *does*, so that taking one early, late, or constantly has an outcome; that is a job for the offline stand-in (`offline_env.py`), which is plumbing today rather than physics. Either way the emulator remains the only arbiter of dynamics, and its run stays the thing an arm has to earn.

corridor); decoder channel arithmetic and a hard-coded zero jump margin (fixed; 313 -> 1247);
per-frame instead of per-cadence supervision (separation 0.24-0.29 vs 2.4-5.8);
progress-only recovery labelling (fixed; 1247 -> 1246, i.e. not the blocker); and training
the visual pathway (recall@0.35 **0.280** vs 0.333 untrained, paired -0.053 on 0/3 replays,
i.e. worse than not training at all, plus an evidence-saturation failure that leaves the
shipped margin unable to separate the chunk classes); and teacher-forced sequential scoring
itself as a *predictor* of the emulator's ordering (on the reserved three, round 2 0.850 vs round
1 0.817 recall while the emulator has round 1 ahead 1247 vs 899; on the dev five the recall
ordering *flips* to round 1 ahead -- so the view cannot be relied on to rank arms, though it
remains the gate and it is what caught the always-jump exploit); and the motor readout as the
whole explanation of the completion gap (the evidence's *own* ceiling is 0.656 bounded per-jump
recall at the shipped window and 0.928-0.952 at twenty draws, both short of the 0.97 completion
needs, while a held-out readout of the population one layer upstream is worse at every window);
and the settle-window statistic itself (the motor drive, continuous where the spike sum is
six-valued, is worth 0.160 vs 0.056 bounded recall on the ceiling's own fitted-boundary metric and
wins the untrained arm 0.960 on the sequential view -- but at a 0.374 jump rate, and the rate-free
budget view cannot separate the three statistics to within 0.02, so it is a different operating
point on the same ROC and not more evidence).
Every one of those was a real defect or a real design question, and none of them was the gate.

The measurement is fixed, and the remaining constraint has been *priced* rather than assumed: at
the shipped window the evidence's ceiling is 0.656 bounded per-jump recall where the shipped sum
achieves 0.04, but the one causal rule that reaches further into that gap (the analog drive, 0.160)
was tried through the gate and bought nothing rate-free while costing 0.30 of sequential recall, so
the decoder-at-the-shipped-window margin is now spent. What is left is the ceiling that only reaches
0.928-0.952 (AUC 0.99) when a decision is allowed twenty draws -- the window -- and behind it the
representation, with a number to hit: 0.97 per-jump reliability. The sequential view is the
third attempt and the most honest: it measures the right thing (decisions made in context). Its
ranking of round 1 against round 2 is **not stable** -- round 2 ahead on the reserved three
(0.850 vs 0.817, against an emulator that has round 1 ahead), round 1 ahead on the dev five
(0.840 vs 0.810, matching it) -- so it must not be used to rank arms at this sample size. What
*is* structural: with the frames teacher-forced, an arm's own trajectory never exists, so no
offline replay can model the state its decisions would create. The emulator stays the only
arbiter of dynamics; the pre-screen's job is to stop wasting runs on arms that are wrong for
reasons it *can* see. Three numbers this plan leaned
on do not survive their own error bars: the "~0.66-0.70" readout ceiling is 0.541 at the epochs
the runner actually uses (it needs 10-30 epochs), an untrained model scores the same as a
trained one, and per-replay variance is *larger* than any arm's paired gain. What the new
pre-screen adds is a falsifiable statement of where the arms actually are: 13-16 points above
their own untrained baseline at catching the teacher's jumps inside a 35% jump budget (dev
seeds: 0.496 and 0.504 against 0.360 untrained and 0.343 chance), which is still far short of
what 100 sequential decisions needs. Pre-screening remains the right discipline — it takes seconds and caught
the 313 -> 1247 decoder defect — and its output is now a *paired, multi-seed, per-replay*
comparison with a trivial baseline next to it, never a single calibrated number.

One honest limit stays on the record: on the reserved three the pre-screen ranked round 2
(0.850 recall, closed-loop 899) above round 1 (0.817, closed-loop 1247), and on the dev five it
reverses to round 1 ahead (0.840 vs 0.810) -- so the ranking is not stable enough to select arms
by, only to gate them. It is a gate and a diagnostic, not a surrogate for the emulator -- and
the arms it is gating are all currently on the wrong side of it. Report completion rate, Wilson lower bound, best
X, death rate, divergence-window counts and provenance (`model_only`, assistance flags, ROM
and dataset checksums) for every arm. Do not treat bootstrap-assisted or synthetic-env
numbers as learning evidence, and do not call a cadence change a learned policy.
